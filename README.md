# CS-230-Operating-Platforms
Portfolio project from CS 230 Operating Platforms showcasing my software design document for The Gaming Room.

## Project Reflection

### 1. Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements.

The Gaming Room wanted to expand their game, Draw It or Lose It, from Android to other platforms. They needed a web-based version that could support multiple players and teams while keeping everything secure and running smoothly.

### 2. What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?

I think I did a good job comparing the different operating systems and explaining why Linux would be a good option. I also made sure to consider things like security, memory, storage, and costs instead of focusing on just one thing.

### 3. What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?

Working on the design document helped me see how important it is to plan things out before jumping into coding. It gave me a better understanding of what the application needed and how everything would work together. I think having that information figured out ahead of time would make the actual development process easier.

### 4. If you could choose one part of your work to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could go back and improve one part, I would probably focus more on security. I included some recommendations, but I think I could have explained more about how those security measures would actually be put into place and tested.

### 5. How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it important to consider the user's needs?

I looked at what The Gaming Room wanted from the application, like supporting multiple players, working across different platforms, and keeping user information secure. I used those needs to help decide which operating system and design would work best. I think it's important to understand what the user actually wants because there's no point in building something that doesn't meet their needs.

### 6. How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future?

I started by looking at the client's requirements and then comparing the different options available. I considered the pros and cons of each operating system before making my recommendations. In the future, I would use a similar approach, but I would also spend more time thinking about testing and what problems could come up along the way.
